Lullabyte

How a name becomes a lullaby: we start with how your name sounds, not how it's spelled. Each syllable becomes a note. Its vowel places it on the keys: rounder, deeper vowels tend lower; brighter front vowels rise higher. The consonant before it nudges the note and shapes how it arrives, from soft and flowing to crisp and struck. Stressed syllables linger. Gliding vowels become two notes. Every pitch settles into a five-note scale, C · D · E · G · A, which keeps even unfamiliar combinations gently consonant. Same pronunciation, same lullaby; a different pronunciation, a different song. And when the name is finished, one last low C rings out. That one's ours.
